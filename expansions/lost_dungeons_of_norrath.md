## Overview
![lost_dungeons_of_norrath_wayfarers.jpg](/lost_dungeons_of_norrath_wayfarers.jpg =350x)

*EverQuest: Lost Dungeons of Norrath* (LDoN) is EverQuest's sixth expansion, and the last one considered part of the Planes of Power era.[^7] It brought widespread instanced dungeons to Norrath: groups take adventures from the Wayfarers, an adventuring organization of NPCs, and each group gets its own copy of the dungeon, scaled to its strength.

The dungeons were discovered by the Wayfarers Brotherhood, a band of adventurers led by a rogue named Morden Rasp, who are also credited with discovering augments.[^7] The expansion's story picks up at Broken Skull Rock, from the previous expansion, The Legacy of Ykesha, as explorers push into the unexplored depths of Norrath's dungeons. The rediscovery of forgotten dungeons, artifacts and power sets Norrath's rival factions against each other. To control the newly found areas, they hire mercenaries and adventurers, and at times pit them against one another.[^2]

### Announcement and Release
Sony Online Entertainment announced Lost Dungeons of Norrath in San Diego on June 16, 2003, as a full-sized expansion built around more than forty dungeons and focused mainly on content for a single group.[^2] Michael Lustenberger, SOE's director of product marketing, said the expansion was "putting the emphasis of the game squarely on the classic fantasy environment: the dungeon." SOE promised hundreds of new quests, with environments suited to players of all levels and play styles. The announcement set a fall 2003 release, both as a download and in limited quantities at retail.

It was released on September 9, 2003, as a digital download, with a boxed edition sold at retail.[^4][^5]

| --- | --- | --- |

**Retail box extras:** the boxed edition included a small round-fobbed keychain and a claim item, the **Satchel of Abu-Kar**: an 8-slot bag with 25% weight reduction. An in-game quest upgrades it to the **Reinforced Satchel of Abu-Kar**, a 10-slot bag with 50% weight reduction.[^4] This began Sony's practice of offering in-game items as an incentive to buy the retail edition of an expansion.[^1]

## Key Features

**Instanced Adventures** - Each Wayfarer camp offers adventures in one of five dungeon themes, each with 8 to 10 separate dungeon maps. You choose the type of adventure and its difficulty, normal or hard, and your group gets its own instance scaled to its average level.[^1][^4] See [How Adventures Work](#how-adventures-work).

**Augments and Slotted Armor** - Augments add to the power of an existing item when placed in one of its augment slots. Existing items in the game generally have only one augment slot, so they can take only one augment; items from the Wayfarer merchants occasionally have up to three.[^1]

**Adventure Points** - Successful adventures award Adventure Points, the game's first alternate currency, spent with specialty merchants at the camps on items, spells and augments.[^1][^4]

**Magus Translocators** - Each camp has a magus who teleports players between the Wayfarer camps. Magus NPCs were introduced with this expansion.[^1][^4]

**Adventure Statistics** - An in-game window tracks each player's adventure wins and losses, which Bonzz describes as the first and only in-game ranking system.[^4]

**Raids** - The camps also offer raid adventures, where multiple groups take on difficult high-end content.[^1] Raids allow 6 to 54 players.[^4] See [Raids](#raids).

## How Adventures Work
Request an adventure from the Wayfarer at a theme's camp, travel to the dungeon entrance it names, and enter your group's instance.[^1]

Adventures come in three request types:

* **Boss:** kill a specific named NPC.
* **Kill count:** kill a set number of the dungeon's inhabitants.
* **Collect:** gather a set number of a specific item.

>You will never be offered any of the last five instances you took part in, whether or not you completed them. For example, if you were sent to kill Boss Z in takish_a, you will not be offered that exact adventure again until you have accepted five other adventures. You could still be offered Boss Z in takish_c, or Boss Y in takish_a. Hard and normal appear to count as different instances, so the same adventure may be offered in both modes within five adventures of each other.
{.is-warning}

**Merchants:** each camp has Adventure merchants selling augments, spells and armor specific to that theme, along with items available at every camp.[^1] Adventure Points can be spent at any camp, but each merchant only sells items up to the number of points you have earned in that camp's theme, revealing more items as you earn more.[^4] Points don't appear in the Alternate Currency tab. Press Alt+V to open the Adventure window and click **View Stats** to see your points in each theme, a grand total, and your wins and losses.[^4]

<!-- TODO: link to a merchant item listing -->

**Choosing your dungeon:** if you are working on exploration or hunter achievements, you can steer which dungeon you get. A kill-count adventure's offer text states how many kills it requires and which entrance to use, and that combination usually identifies the dungeon. Request a normal kill-count adventure, read the offer before accepting it, and if it isn't a dungeon you need, wait about a minute and request another.[^4] Each theme's kill counts are listed under [Exploring the Lost Dungeons](#exploring-the-lost-dungeons). Some combinations can lead to more than one dungeon.

**Known adventures:** each dungeon variation is only offered for certain kinds of adventure. The Known Adventures lists for each theme collect what is known so far from various sources and are incomplete.[^1]

## Exploring the Lost Dungeons
Each theme has a Wayfarer camp, where you take adventures, and one or more dungeon entrances elsewhere in the world. Each dungeon variation is a separate zone with its own map; the zone short names are the ones used by the game files and by EQEmu, and each theme's Dungeons table lists them in EQEmu's zone order.[^6] The complete list of zones was compiled by Allakhazam Volunteer Admin Veludeus.[^1]

>Sources disagree on two dungeon entrances. Allakhazam puts the Rujarkian Hills entrance in North Ro and the Takish-Hiz entrance in South Ro.[^1] Bonzz describes the Rujarkian Hills entrance as a cave in South Ro and the Takish-Hiz entrance as a sand pit in North Ro.[^4] These need to be checked in game.
{.is-warning}

>For six of the 48 maps, EQEmu uses a raid name instead of a dungeon name: *The Curse Reborn*, *Frozen Nightmare*, *Struggles within the Progeny*, *Scion Lair of Fury*, *Prison Break* and *Within the Compact*. Raids appear to reuse the group dungeon maps, so these maps probably also carry the dungeon names that otherwise have no EQEmu match. Those pairings are marked *unconfirmed*, and short names derived from them are marked with a question mark.
{.is-warning}

## Progression and the Adventurer's Stone
At the start, players complete a small task that rewards the **Adventurer's Stone**, a charm augment with no stats.

Every completed adventure awards Adventure Points to spend at the camps, and also counts toward your progress in that theme. After enough adventures, NPCs at the camps may have new dialogue that explains the story behind their theme in some detail. Triggering enough of this dialogue adds bonuses to the Adventurer's Stone. Each theme has a set number of these triggers, so you need to complete a certain number of adventures in every camp to give the Adventurer's Stone its full stats. Once fully progressed, the stone can be converted into the **Wayfarer's Brotherhood Emblem**.[^4]

## Achievements
Achievements were added to EverQuest with the Underfoot expansion, and Lost Dungeons of Norrath has 114 of them, worth 2,150 points in total.[^4]

| Category | Achievements | Points | What they cover |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General | 3 | 80 | Lost Dungeons of Norrath Explorer (10 points), Dungeon Slayer (30) and Conqueror of the Lost Dungeons of Norrath (40), each for completing five listed achievements |
| Exploration | 53 | 530 | A Traveler achievement for visiting each of the 48 dungeons, and an Explorer achievement for each theme |
| Raids | 5 | 50 | A Conqueror achievement for each theme, for defeating its raids |
| Hunter | 53 | 1,490 | A Hunter achievement for each dungeon (30 points each, for defeating between 3 and 30 listed targets), and one for each theme (10 points each) |

**Tip:** many named NPCs appear in more than one dungeon's Hunter achievement. Killing one in any LDoN dungeon counts for every Hunter achievement that lists it, so you only need to kill each one once.[^4] To get the dungeons you need for Traveler and Hunter achievements, see **Choosing your dungeon** under [How Adventures Work](#how-adventures-work).
